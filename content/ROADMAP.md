# Roadmap

Every chapter is planned here before anyone writes it. Each row is the brief for one chapter: an agent writing chapter N reads its row and does not decide the idea, the place or the story beat itself. Rows are written so that several chapters can be written at the same time by different agents, without waiting for each other.

Status: `planned` (row is ready) → `drafted` (an agent wrote it and the build passes) → `released` (the owner played it and published it; frozen from then on).

## Chapter briefs

Each brief gives: where the chapter happens, who it is about, what the player helps with, the new idea, the science reveal, and the question the chapter's `end` leaves open. The last level's `story.next` leads into the next row's place.

### Book 2: The Lantern Road (c4-c8)

Book question: *Where did the Old Keeper go?*

**c4 The lantern road.** The Great Lantern's light shows a road leading out of Deepvan, lined with dark waystones. Travellers are arriving along it in the dark, tired and lost. At the first bend, five groups of travellers wait in different spots; one Sunbell must reach them all. New creature: **Chanda**, a firefly who has walked the road and acts as the player's road guide for book 2. Idea: one source can feed many places through branching wires. Reveal: *"You built a power grid"*: one power station feeds a whole town through branching wires. End question: the first waystone lights, and inside it is a scrap of paper with the lantern mark. Did the Old Keeper come this way?

**c5 Reeds by the road.** Toads sleep in the ditches along the road, and the light must pass them without waking them. Idea: light spills onto the soil beside a wire, so a wire has to keep its distance or hide behind rocks. Reveal: *"You insulated your wires"*: wires are wrapped in plastic so the current goes only where it should. End question: the second waystone holds another scrap: "the bees remember me".

**c6 The beehive hollow.** A hollow in an old tree, where Glowmoss grows in the shape of honeycomb. The bees want moss shapes that stay still for the winter. Idea: moss shapes that never change: beehive, loaf, boat, tub, block. Reveal: *"You found still lifes"*: in the Game of Life, shapes that never change are called still lifes, and the beehive, loaf and boat are their real names. End question: the bees say the Old Keeper crossed a bridge "that needs two hands to open".

**c7 Two-armed bridge.** A bridge that lowers only when its two lanterns glow, one on each arm. Idea: one Sunbell's light has to split into two paths that meet again at a Twinlily. Reveal: *"You copied a signal"*: in a computer, one signal is split by wires and fed into many gates at once. End question: past the bridge, the fifth waystone is silent; it lights only when something is *not* there.

**c8 The quiet waystone.** The waystone at the end of book 2's land. It blooms in the dark: its Shadeferns flower only when the lights beside them are not both on. Mixed chapter: slots 3 and 4 are life levels that review c6 and c3. Idea: a Shadefern beside a Twinlily blooms unless both of the Twinlily's lights are on (NAND). Reveal: *"You built a NAND gate"*: every calculation a computer does can be built from NAND gates alone, and phone memory chips are named after it. Book answer and end question: the Old Keeper's first full letter: "I've gone to the Glass River. Its lights go out every night, and I want to know why."

### Book 3: The Glass River (c9-c13)

Book question: *Why do the river's lights go out every night?* New guide creature allowed: one river creature, introduced in c9.

**c9 Ripples.** The riverbank, where moss grows in rings like ripples and flickers night by night. Idea: shapes that repeat every two nights (blinker, toad, beacon); the number of nights decides which phase the beetle sees. Reveal: *"You found oscillators"*: the clock in a computer is an oscillator that ticks billions of times a second. End question: the lights on the river are fine at dusk, but gone by morning.

**c10 Where the sun sits.** Stepping stones across a shallow ford. The player gets the Sunbells in the pouch and must decide where the light should come from. Idea: placing the source is part of the puzzle. Reveal: *"You placed the power stations"*: engineers choose where power stations and phone towers go so one source serves the most people. End question: a ferry that should carry the lights across has stopped.

**c11 The ferry crossing.** The ferry is pulled by walking moss. Idea: gliders travel across the board, one step every 4 nights; aim them, and use rocks to stop them in the right place. Reveal: *"You sent gliders"*: scientists have built entire working computers in the Game of Life, sending gliders as signals. End question: on the far bank, still pools reflect the lights, and the reflections don't match.

**c12 Mirror pools.** Pools where every light needs a twin. Idea: Twinlilies fed by other Twinlilies: gates feeding gates. Reveal: *"You built a circuit of gates"*: an adding machine is gates wired into gates; that is how a calculator adds. End question: downstream, the glass waystone flickers out every night. Why?

**c13 The glass waystone.** Book 3's last waystone, on an island. The river washes the moss away each night. Mixed chapter: slots 3 and 4 are light levels that review c10 and c12. Idea: making moss vanish exactly where the toads want it gone, by choosing shapes that die out. Reveal: *"You found patterns that fade"*: in the Game of Life most random shapes die out, and scientists study which ones survive. Book answer and end question: the river carries light away; waystones hold it. And from the hills, a light blinks back, like a signal.

### Book 4: The Echo Hills (c14-c18)

Book question: *Who is signalling from the hills?* New guide creature allowed: one hill creature, introduced in c14.

**c14 The long climb.** A long, winding path up the hills with very few seeds to spare. Idea: the fewest Reeds that reach every traveller. Bigger boards (7x7 or 8x8). Reveal: *"You found the shortest network"*: the cables under cities and oceans are laid out so the fewest kilometres reach the most places. End question: the blinking light is coming from inside a cave.

**c15 Echo caves.** Caves where the moss echoes old patterns. Some moss is already growing (`fixed` moss); add one or two moss to change what it becomes. Idea: a tiny change makes a big difference. Reveal: *"You found the butterfly effect"*: in many systems one small change grows into a big one; weather is like this. End question: the cave lamp blinks in a pattern. A message?

**c16 Snow lilies.** A frozen meadow where Twinlilies grow under the snow. The pouch has more seeds than needed, and some are tempting. Idea: not every seed should be planted; the simplest circuit wins. Reveal: *"You kept it simple"*: engineers remove every part that isn't needed, because each one can fail. End question: a rockslide has blocked the path to the summit.

**c17 The rockslide.** Rocks scattered across the moss beds. Idea: rocks shape how moss grows: they block births, split patterns and stop gliders. Reveal: *"You built with walls"*: in the Game of Life, fixed walls let scientists steer patterns, the way pipes steer water. End question: the summit waystone is the blinking light, and it blinks the same on both sides.

**c18 The summit waystone.** The top of the hills, with two matching halves. Mixed chapter: slots 3 and 4 are symmetric life levels that review c15 and c17. Idea: symmetric boards where one half's answer mirrors the other's. Reveal: *"You used symmetry"*: chips, buildings and snowflakes are built from repeated, mirrored pieces. Book answer and end question: the blinking was the Old Keeper's message-lamp. Its last message: "Look up."

### Book 5: The Sky Orchard (c19-c23)

Book question: *Where do new lights come from?* The owner decides the rest of book 5 before c21 is planned.

**c19 Star seeds.** Trees so tall their fruit are stars, and some have fallen dark. Sunbells are in the pouch and Shadeferns guard the orchard. Idea: choosing the source and keeping ferns in the dark at once. Reveal: *"You designed a whole circuit"*: sources, wires and gates together; that is what a circuit designer does. End question: the clouds over the orchard are made of moss.

**c20 Cloud moss.** Moss drifting through the sky like clouds. Idea: gliders that crash into still lifes; some crashes destroy, some build. Reveal: *"You made patterns collide"*: in the Game of Life, collisions are how patterns compute. End question: decided when c21 is planned.

## After c20

To plan the next book (5 chapters), the owner (or an agent asked to plan, not to write levels) adds one book section and five rows, following the same pattern:

1. The book's land, question and answer, added to the Books table in STORY_BIBLE.md. The answer opens the next land.
2. Five chapters: 2 or 3 light, 1 or 2 life, and the last one mixed. Never two life chapters in a row.
3. Each chapter gets one idea: an unused one from the catalogue first. When none are left, use a pair of used ideas (like "L-SOURCE + L-NAND") that has not been paired before.
4. A new idea can be added to the catalogue only if it can be built from the existing plants and rules. A new plant or rule is engine work for the owner, never part of a chapter brief.

## Release plan

- **Launch:** c4 to c20 are written (they can be written in parallel, one agent per chapter), played by the owner, and released together. That makes 20 chapters and 97 levels.
- **After launch:** one chapter every 10 to 15 days. Always keep at least 2 chapters `drafted` ahead of release, so a missed week never leaves players waiting.
- **Every release:** the next planned chapter is shown in the game as a locked "Up next" teaser with its arrival date, so the newest chapter never looks like the last.

## Making the game feel endless (owner, game code)

The content rules above keep chapters coming. These changes to the game itself stop it from ever *looking* finished. They are engine and UI work, not content, and should land before c4 is released.

1. **No finale on the newest chapter.** Today, finishing the last chapter in chapters.json shows "You are the Keeper of Deepvan". Instead, show "The road goes on" with the next waystone's name and arrival date.
2. **A "coming" entry in chapters.json** (name, Hinglish name, date, no file) that the game shows as the locked Up next chip, and that the build accepts without a chapter file.
3. **No total spark count.** "5 of 12 sparks" and "collect all 12 to light it" tell the player where the game ends. Count sparks per book or per waystone instead, and change the tour line.
4. **Ranks that keep growing.** One rank per book (Keeper of the Lantern Road, Keeper of the Glass River...), so there is always a next title.
5. **A road map** with the lit waystones and one or two dark ones fading into fog beyond the newest.
6. **While you wait.** After the newest chapter, lead the player to Today's grove and Wandering visitors, which are already endless.
