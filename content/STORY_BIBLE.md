# Story bible

The fixed facts of Deepvan. Every chapter must agree with this file. Add to it only where [AUTHORING.md](AUTHORING.md) says you may (a new creature, a "Story so far" line); everything else is the owner's to change.

## The promise

A calm quest. Nothing is ever lost, nobody is a villain, nobody is in danger, and the player can stop anywhere. The game teaches real science (circuits, logic, cellular automata) through plants the player learns by watching, and names the real idea only after the player has worked it out.

## Why the story never ends

The Great Lantern is lit again (end of chapter 3). Its light shows the **Lantern Road**: a road of small lanterns called **waystones** (Hinglish: *raahdeep*) that leads out of Deepvan and on through far lands. Every waystone on the road has gone dark.

- Each **chapter** lights one waystone, in one new place along the road.
- Each **book** of 5 chapters crosses one land, and answers one question about it.
- The **Old Keeper** walked this road before the player, and left letters at the waystones. The player is always one waystone behind. They never meet. Each book ends with a letter that sends the player on into the next land.

So the road can always go on: there is always a next waystone, a next land and a next letter.

## Books

Each book asks one question in its first chapter and answers it in its last. The answer always opens the next book's question.

| Book | Chapters | Land | Question | Answer (at the book's last chapter) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 The Great Lantern | c1-c3 | Deepvan | Why did the Great Lantern go dark? | The Old Keeper let it rest so a new keeper could learn to light it. The player lights it. |
| 2 The Lantern Road | c4-c8 | The road out of Deepvan | Where did the Old Keeper go? | A letter at the fifth waystone: the Old Keeper went to the Glass River, because its lights go out every night. |
| 3 The Glass River | c9-c13 | A wide, slow river | Why do the river's lights go out every night? | The river carries light away downstream; the river creatures learn to hold it with waystones. A light blinks back from the hills, like a signal. |
| 4 The Echo Hills | c14-c18 | Hills with caves and snow | Who is signalling from the hills? | The Old Keeper's blinking message-lamps. The last one says: "Look up." |
| 5 The Sky Orchard | c19-c23 | An orchard so high the stars hang in it | Where do new lights come from? | Decided by the owner when book 5 is planned. |

## Story so far

One line per chapter, written by whoever writes the chapter. Read all of it before writing a new one.

- **c1 First light:** Four witnesses talk: Pinku saw a shadow on the tower, Mendhu heard "Someone new will come", a page with a lantern mark is found, and the owl's map points to the deep garden.
- **c2 Flowers that decide:** In the deep garden, Dheeru gives up the silver key, a page says "I did not break the lantern", and the key opens the mossy gate.
- **c3 Moss that remembers:** The moss shows the Old Keeper's footprints, walking moss leads to the last page, and the Great Lantern is lit again. Visitors keep arriving from far away.
- **c4 The lantern road:** Chanda guides the new Keeper to the first dark waystone; it holds a scrap marked with the Old Keeper's lantern, raising the question of whether the Old Keeper walked this road.
- **c5 Reeds by the road:** The new Keeper carries light past sleeping toads to the second waystone, where Chanda finds a note: "The bees remember me."
- **c6 The beehive hollow:** The bees show the new Keeper moss shapes that stay through winter, then recall the Old Keeper crossing toward a bridge that opens with light on both sides.
